What the consistency rule means

A consistency rule caps how much of your total profit can come from your single best day. Firms use it so nobody passes on one lucky, oversized session. FTMO calls its version the Best Day Rule; futures firms usually say "consistency" or "consistency target".

It's written as a percentage:

Best day profit ÷ total profit ≤ consistency %

Flip it around and you get the number that actually matters:

Minimum total profit needed = best day ÷ consistency %

Worked example

A 50K evaluation with a $3,000 target and a 50% consistency rule. Your days so far: +$400, +$1,800, −$300, +$600. Total profit is $2,500 and your best day is $1,800.

  • $1,800 ÷ $2,500 = 72%, which is above 50%, so you don't meet consistency yet.
  • Needed total = $1,800 ÷ 0.50 = $3,600.
  • The target says $3,000, but you really need $1,100 more. And no day can beat $1,800 while you get there, or the bar moves up again.

How to pass without fighting the rule

  1. Cap your daily profit, not just your loss. With a 50% rule and a $3,000 target, any day over $1,500 raises the bar. Many traders stop for the day at about 30–40% of the target.
  2. Check the ratio before you size up. If you already had a big day, the cheapest fix is several medium days, not another home run.
  3. Remember the rule usually applies to payouts too. On funded accounts a single huge day can delay a withdrawal the same way.
  4. Log every day's P&L per account. The ratio is easy to work out on one account and easy to lose track of on four. Our journal loads each account's consistency rule from its firm and keeps daily P&L per account on a calendar, so the check takes seconds.

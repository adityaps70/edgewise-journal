Step 1: Turn the rules into numbers

Before your first trade, write down four numbers for the account:

  • Target: e.g. $3,000 on a 50K.
  • Max loss and its type: e.g. $2,000 end-of-day trailing. If you're unsure what that means, read trailing drawdown explained.
  • Daily loss limit: the firm's, if any; otherwise you'll set your own.
  • Consistency rule: e.g. 50%, meaning no day can be more than half your total profit. See the consistency rule guide.

Our 50K rules comparison has these for the main futures and forex firms.

Step 2: Size from your room, not your account

A "50K" account is really a $2,000 account with a $3,000 target. Size from the $2,000.

  • Risk per trade: 10–15% of remaining room. With $2,000 of room, that's $200–$300.
  • When room shrinks to $1,200 after losses, risk drops to $120–$180 automatically.
  • That means about 7–10 consecutive full losses before a breach, rather than 3–4.

Step 3: Set a daily stop inside the firm's limit

Most breaches happen on one bad day, after the third or fourth loss, when a trader sizes up to get it back. A personal daily stop ends that day before it ends the account.

  • Daily stop: about 25% of room, e.g. $500 on a fresh 50K.
  • Or a trade-count stop: done after two losing trades in a row.
  • If the firm has a daily limit (e.g. $1,250), your stop still sits well inside it.

Step 4: Cap your good days too

With a 50% consistency rule and a $3,000 target, any day over $1,500 makes the target harder. With trailing drawdown, a huge day also drags the floor up. A daily profit goal of roughly 15–25% of the target ($450–$750) keeps both rules quiet. Hit it, and stop.

Step 5: Only take your best setups

An evaluation isn't the place to test ideas. Grade every setup before you enter:

  • A+: your best, most-tested pattern, with every condition met. Full risk.
  • B: valid but missing something. Half risk.
  • C: marginal. Skip it during an evaluation.

After a few weeks your journal will show what each grade actually earns. For most traders the C trades are where the money leaks.

Step 6: Review every day, in five minutes

  1. Did I follow the daily stop and profit cap?
  2. What grade was each trade, and did the grade match the result?
  3. How do I feel: calm, rushed, revenge-y? Rate it from 0 to 10.
  4. How much room do I have tomorrow?

For a full list of what to record, see what to write in a trading journal.

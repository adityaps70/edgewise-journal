What to write in a trading journal: a template with examples

By Aditya Pratap Singh · 2026-11-23 · 7 min read

A journal only helps if it records the things you can actually change. Below: what to write, a template you can paste into a spreadsheet, and how to review it each week.

The fields that matter

The trade

  • Date and time (and session: London, New York AM, etc.)
  • Instrument and direction: NQ long, EURUSD short
  • Entry, stop, target, exit
  • Size and P&L in dollars and in R (P&L ÷ initial risk)

The decision

  • Setup name: "opening range break", "VWAP reclaim"
  • Grade: A+, B or C, assigned before entry
  • Reason: one sentence on why
  • Followed plan? yes / no, and if no, what broke

The trader

  • State before entry: calm, bored, rushed, angry, FOMO
  • Mindset score 0–10: how detached and patient you were
  • Exit quality: planned exit, early fear exit, moved stop

For prop firm accounts, add

  • Account and firm
  • Room left after the trade (balance minus drawdown floor; see trailing drawdown explained)
  • Day's P&L vs the consistency limit (see consistency rule)

Copyable template

Paste this header row into Excel or Google Sheets:

Date | Session | Account | Instrument | Long/Short | Entry | Stop | Target | Exit | Size | P&L $ | R | Setup | Grade | Reason | Followed plan | State | Mindset 0-10 | Exit quality | Room left | Screenshot

Daily: before and after

Before the open (3 minutes): key levels, news times, max loss for the day, which setups you're allowed to take.

After the close (5 minutes): did you follow the daily stop, best and worst decision, mindset score, room left for tomorrow.

Weekly review: where the edge comes from

  1. Total P&L by setup: which ones pay and which leak?
  2. P&L by grade: do A+ trades really outperform C trades?
  3. P&L by time of day and state: when and how do you lose?
  4. Pick one rule for next week, e.g. "stop after two losses" or "no C setups".

FAQ

What should I write in my trading journal?

For each trade: date, instrument, direction, entry, stop, target, exit, size, P&L in dollars and R, the setup name and grade, why you took it, how you felt before and after, whether you followed your plan, and a screenshot. Once a day add a short pre-market plan and an evening review.

What is the best trading journal format?

One row per trade with fixed columns works best, because you can filter and total it. Keep free-text notes short and put screenshots in a linked folder or a journal app. Excel or Google Sheets is fine to start; a dedicated journal saves time once you trade several accounts.

How often should I review my trading journal?

Briefly every day after the close, and properly once a week. The weekly review is where you group trades by setup, time and emotion and decide one thing to change next week.
